If the device will come out of a pocket all day for a photo, a message, a fault code or a parts label, buy the handheld. On Android that is the Tuga A6 at £299. If the screen is doing the work, which means long forms, drawings, quotes walked through at a kitchen table or a spreadsheet, buy a tablet: the Tuga A8 at £379 if you need to hold it in one hand, the Tuga A10 at £449 if you need to read a plan without zooming.

That is the short answer. The harder question is whether you need one device or two. It is easy to buy a tablet, keep using your personal phone for everything else, and end up with the tablet in the van and the phone doing the job. It is just as easy to buy a handheld and spend the year squinting at a form built for a desktop. Getting the shape right matters more than any single spec.

What We Mean by Rugged Phone

A rugged phone is a pocket sized device for site work: something that takes photos in bad light and survives knocks, drops and weather. The A6 is the Tuga Android handheld in that size class (the Windows WH6 is covered below). It is a 6.78 inch Android 14 handheld with 5G, Wi-Fi 5, Bluetooth 5.2 and NFC, sealed to IP68 and IP69K and rated MIL-STD-810H.

The A6 is a handheld with 5G. If making and taking calls on the device matters to you, ask us before you order.

The Screen Question

Screen size is the one thing you cannot change later, so start there. Three sizes, three different jobs.

6.78 Inches: The A6

The A6 screen is 2460 x 1080 at 120Hz, sharper than either tablet on paper. Physical size is the issue, not sharpness. An app built for a phone is comfortable on 6.78 inches. A PDF drawing, a desktop web app with a sidebar, or a form with dozens of fields is not. You can, with pinching and scrolling, but not all day.

8 Inches: The A8

The A8 screen is 1280 x 800 and runs at 800 nits, against 560 on the A6 and 350 on the A10. Eight inches is the size at which a long form, a route or a quote with pictures becomes readable without zooming, and it is still small enough to hold in one hand on a ladder or drop into a van door pocket.

10.1 Inches: The A10

The A10 screen is 10.1 inches at 1920 x 1200, Full HD. This is the one for drawings, BIM viewers, full spreadsheets and PDF plans, where you need the whole page at once. The catch is brightness. The A10 is rated at 350 nits against 800 on the A8 and 560 on the A6, so it is at its best in a cab, a site office or under cover rather than on an open roof in July. If most of your reading happens in direct sun, the A8 is the better outdoor screen even though it is smaller.

The Pocket Question

The A6 weighs 441g. Both tablets weigh 680g. What matters is where the device lives. A handheld goes in a pocket or a belt holster, and it is still there when you climb into a loft or lean over a trench. A tablet lives in the van, on a dashboard mount, in a bag or in your hand. When you need both hands for a parcel or a ladder, a tablet has to be put down somewhere, which is an extra risk for any device.

Specs That Are Not About Size

The smallest device is not the weakest one. On several specs the A6 leads the three.

  • Camera. The A6 has a 64MP night vision camera plus a 50MP main camera. The A8 and A10 have a 13MP rear camera and a 5MP front camera, the A10 with a flash. If photographing fuse boards, voids and lofts is a big part of your day, the A6 is the one to choose, with its 64MP night vision camera against 13MP on the A8 and A10.
  • Connectivity. The A6 has 5G. The A8 has 4G LTE. The A10 has 4G with dual SIM, so you can carry two networks for patchy rural coverage.
  • Battery. The A6 has a 10600mAh battery with 33W fast charging, the A8 10000mAh with 18W fast charging, the A10 8000mAh with quick charge.
  • Memory and storage. The A6 has 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, expandable by microSD up to 2TB. The A8 and A10 have 8GB and 128GB, expandable to 512GB on the A8 and 2TB on the A10.
  • Sealing. All three are IP68 and MIL-STD-810H. Only the A6 adds IP69K, which covers high pressure, high temperature water jets, so it is the only one of the three rated for pressure washing. Our IP ratings guide explains the difference.
  • Gloves and unlocking. Glove mode is listed on the A6 and A8, not the A10. The A6 has a fingerprint sensor and face unlock. Neither tablet lists either.
  • Ports. The A8 has USB-C and USB-A. The A10 has USB-C, a pogo pin connector for docking and a 3.5mm headphone jack. The A6 has USB-C with OTG. No USB-A port is listed for the A6 or A10, so a USB-A peripheral needs the USB-C to USB-A OTG Adapter at £8.
  • Operating temperature. All three are rated to operate from -20°C to 60°C.

A tablet costs you more than pocket size. Against the A6 you give up the 64MP night vision camera, 5G and the 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, and the A10 has a smaller battery too. In return you get screen size, plus 800 nit brightness on the A8 and dual SIM on the A10. A fair trade if the screen is what you need. A poor one if it is not.

One Device or Two?

This is the real decision. There are three ways to set it up.

One Handheld for Work

The A6 at £299 is the lowest priced device in our range and the one built to live in a pocket, so it is the one to consider if you want one pocketable work device rather than a tablet that stays in the van. If you want it to replace your personal phone, ask us about calling first. If your job sheets, invoices, photos and messages all live in phone apps, you may never miss the bigger screen. This may suit sole traders doing domestic work, delivery drivers and anyone on their feet all day.

One Tablet Plus the Phone You Already Have

If you are happy with your personal phone for calls and messages and simply need a work screen that survives site, buy the A8 or A10 and leave the phone in your pocket. This suits electricians filling in test schedules, surveyors and site managers reading plans, and field service engineers running a job queue from a vehicle mount. The weak point is that your personal phone is then doing the site photos.
