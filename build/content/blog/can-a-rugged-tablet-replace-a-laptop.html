For most sole traders, yes. If the laptop's job is quotes, invoices, email, job sheets, spreadsheets, PDF drawings and the odd supplier portal, a rugged tablet can do all of it, and it will survive the van and the site in a way a laptop never will. The honest exceptions are heavy CAD, video editing, big 3D models and any program that only runs on a full Windows PC with a proper graphics card. If that is your day, keep the laptop. If it is not, read on, because the question is really which kind of tablet replaces it, and that comes down to one test.

The Short Version

  • Everything you do runs in a browser or a phone app? An Android tablet replaces the laptop for less money. The Tuga A10 at £449 gives you a 10.1 inch Full HD screen, and the Tuga A8 at £379 an 8 inch one.
  • One program you cannot drop only runs on Windows? You need a Windows tablet. The Tuga W10 at £649 is the closest thing in our range to a laptop, with real ports. The Tuga W8 at £629 is the smaller, lighter Windows option.
  • Heavy CAD, rendering or video? Neither replaces a workstation laptop. Buy the tablet for the site and keep the laptop for the desk, or skip the tablet.

Start With the Software, Not the Hardware

A laptop is a box that runs programs. Whether a tablet can replace it depends entirely on which programs. Write down every piece of software you use for work in a normal week, then ask of each one whether it runs in a web browser, has an Android app, or needs to be installed on Windows.

If every line runs in a browser or has an Android app, you do not need Windows at all. If one line needs Windows and you cannot replace it, that one line decides the device. Our Android or Windows guide walks through this as a five minute test, and it is worth doing before you look at a single spec.

Two cautions. First, check each app on the actual device, or at least on the same Android version, before you commit. The A10 runs Android 14 and the A8 runs Android 12, and an app that works on one may not be offered for the other. We cannot tell you how a given app performs on our devices, only which operating system they run. Second, "it has a web version" is not the same as "the web version does everything", so try the web version for a week on whatever you have now.

What a Rugged Tablet Does Better Than a Laptop

The case for the tablet is not that it is a better computer. It is that it goes where the laptop will not.

  • It survives the van and the site. Every device in our range is sealed to at least IP65. The A10 is IP68 and tested to MIL-STD-810H. The W8 is IP65, tested to MIL-STD-810G and rated for 1.22 metre drops. A laptop left on a dashboard or a wet bench has none of that.
  • The customer signs on it. Signatures, photos, a quote accepted at the kitchen table. The paperless guide covers how that workflow fits together.
  • It has a camera. Photos go straight into the job record. The A10 and A8 have a 13MP rear camera with flash, the W8 an 8MP rear camera and the W10 a 5MP autofocus rear camera.
  • It gets online on its own. The A10, A8 and W8 have 4G LTE built in, so forms upload from site without a phone hotspot. On the W10, 4G is listed as an option rather than standard, so ask before you order if you need it.
  • It mounts in the vehicle. The Vehicle Mounting Dock at £109 locks the A8, A10, W8 or W10 to a dashboard and keeps it charged. Nobody mounts a laptop on a dashboard.

What a Laptop Still Does Better

Be honest here, because a tablet that cannot replace the laptop ends up as a second device you resent paying for.

  • Typing. A laptop has a keyboard built in. On a tablet you add one. The W10 and W8 have a USB-A port, so a wired keyboard plugs straight in, and every device in the range has Bluetooth for a wireless one. On the Android tablets the £8 USB-C to USB-A OTG Adapter lets a USB keyboard connect.
  • Heavy software. The W10 runs an Intel N100 with 8GB of RAM, and the W8 an Intel Celeron N5100 with 8GB. Those run Windows 11 Pro and ordinary office software. They are not workstation processors, and no device in our range lists a dedicated graphics card. If you render, model in 3D or edit video, a tablet is not the tool.
  • Screen space for two windows. A 10.1 inch screen is fine for one thing at a time. Side by side, a spreadsheet and an email are cramped. The W10 and W8 have HDMI out, which solves this at the desk, as we cover below.
  • Storage. Every device ships with 128GB except the A6 with 64GB. That is less than many laptops. All take a microSD card, and the W10 can be ordered with 256GB or 512GB fitted. The storage guide works out what actually fills a work device.
