Every winter the same thing happens. A tablet that lasted all day in August shows 40% at 10am on a frosty site, then switches itself off at 25%. People assume the battery is failing. Usually, it is not. It is cold.

Rugged tablets are built to work in low temperatures, but the lithium ion battery inside follows the same chemistry as the one in your phone. Knowing how it behaves in the cold, and the one charging mistake to avoid, will get you through the winter without a dead screen or a damaged battery.

Why Batteries Struggle in the Cold

A lithium ion battery produces power through a chemical reaction. Cold slows that reaction down. The battery cannot deliver current as easily, its voltage sags under load, and the device sees less usable capacity than it would on a mild day.

That is why the percentage can drop fast and why a device can shut down while still showing charge. The tablet reads the low voltage as a nearly empty battery and turns off to protect itself.

The good news: this is mostly temporary. Warm the device back up and much of that missing capacity comes back. Using a tablet in the cold does not, on its own, wear the battery out.

The One Thing Not to Do: Charge It While It Is Frozen

Discharging in the cold is fine. Charging in the cold is not.

Charging a lithium ion battery below freezing can cause a process called lithium plating, where metallic lithium builds up inside the cell instead of being stored properly. That damage is permanent. It reduces capacity for the rest of the battery's life and, in severe cases, affects safety. This is why most batteries are specified to charge only above 0°C, and why many devices refuse to charge or charge very slowly when they are cold.

The classic winter mistake: the tablet spends the night in the van at minus three, and first thing in the morning it goes straight onto the charger. Instead:

  • Bring the tablet indoors overnight if you can. It is better for the battery and less tempting to thieves.
  • If it has been in the cold, give it time to reach room temperature before plugging it in.
  • If you charge in the van, do it once the cab has warmed up, not the moment you start the engine.

Check the Operating Temperature

Every rugged device has an operating temperature range on its spec sheet. This is the range the manufacturer says it will work in, and it varies more than you might expect.

  • -20°C to 60°C: Tuga A10, Tuga WH6
  • -20°C to 50°C: Tuga A6
  • -10°C to 50°C: Tuga W8

No operating temperature is listed for the Tuga A8 or the Tuga W10, so we will not guess at one. If you work in hard frost, ask us before you order, or choose a device with a listed range.

A typical UK winter rarely gets near minus twenty, but cold stores, early morning starts in exposed places and Scottish hillsides can drop below minus ten. If you regularly work in those conditions, it is worth choosing a device with the wider range. Remember that the operating range is about the device working, not about charging. The above freezing rule for charging still applies.

Watch Out for Condensation

Bring a cold tablet into a warm, humid room and moisture condenses on it, the same way your glasses steam up. A sealed IP67 or IP68 device keeps that moisture out of its insides, but the charging port and any exposed connectors can still end up damp.

Plugging a charger into a wet port is a bad idea on any device. Give the tablet a few minutes to acclimatise, wipe it down, and make sure the port is dry before you charge. If your device has port covers, keep them closed while it warms up.

Practical Tips for a Full Day in the Cold

Keep it close to your body between uses. An inside jacket pocket keeps a handheld like the A6 or WH6 far warmer than a van seat or a toolbox. For larger tablets, an insulated bag or case helps.

Turn the brightness down. The screen is usually the biggest drain. Sunlight readable displays are designed to cut through summer glare; on a grey December day you rarely need full brightness. Auto brightness does a decent job.

Do not rely on dark mode to save battery. Dark mode saves real power on OLED screens, but most rugged tablets use IPS LCD screens, where the backlight stays on regardless. Turning brightness down makes far more difference.

Close what you are not using. GPS tracking, Bluetooth, constant photo syncing and apps refreshing in the background all draw power. If you are not navigating, you probably do not need location on all day.

Carry a backup. A power bank in a warm pocket, or a USB-C car charger in the van, removes the anxiety completely. Just follow the warm before charging rule.

Consider a swappable battery. The Tuga A6, Tuga A8, Tuga WH6 and Tuga W10 have removable batteries, so a spare kept warm in the cab means you can swap rather than wait.

Plan for gloves. Taking gloves off every time you need the screen gets old quickly in January. None of our devices lists a glove mode, so check how the screen responds through your own work gloves before winter arrives. The A6's two side scan keys do work with gloves on.

Bigger Batteries Buy You Margin

Cold eats into capacity, so the bigger the battery you start with, the more you have left on a bad day. The A8 and A10 each carry 8000mAh, and the A8's is removable. The A6 handheld carries 5050mAh, also removable, so if you have a spare, keep it warm in an inside pocket and swap when the first runs low. Windows tablets have smaller batteries and a more power hungry operating system, so if you work outdoors through the winter on a Windows device, plan to charge it during the day.
