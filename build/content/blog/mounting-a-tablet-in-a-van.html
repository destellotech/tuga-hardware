If the tablet lives in the vehicle, mount it in a locking dock that holds it and charges it, bolted to something solid. If it comes out at every job, a door pocket and a 12V charger do the job for a fraction of the money. For a handheld, a suction mount and the same charger cover it. The rest of this guide explains the choices behind that, what vibration actually does, and how to keep a tablet charged without wearing out its port.

First Decision: Does It Live in the Vehicle or Ride in It?

Work out which of these is you, because it decides how much to spend.

  • It rides in the van. You take the tablet into every job and it sits in a door pocket between them. You do not need a dock.
  • It lives in the vehicle. It is the route screen and job list and stays on the dashboard while you drive. That is what a dock is for.
  • It lives on a machine. A digger, telehandler, tractor or forklift cab. Usually more vibration and less space.

Where to Put It

  • Clear of your view of the road: low on the dashboard, not in the middle of the windscreen.
  • Clear of airbags. Check the vehicle handbook before drilling or sticking anything to a dashboard or pillar.
  • Away from the heater vents and out of the wiper sweep.
  • With the sun in mind. The A8 is listed at 800 nits and the W8 at 700. The A6 is 560, the WH6 400 and the A10 350. The lower figures are harder work with the sun on the screen. The W10 does not list a brightness figure.

What Vibration Does, and What Method 514 Covers

Drops get the attention, but a vehicle mount exposes a tablet to hours of shaking, every working day. Over months, vibration can work connectors loose, fatigue solder joints, back screws out of mounts, creep suction cups and rock a charging plug in its port. Three things reduce it: mount to something stiff, keep the arm short, and avoid a heavy cable hanging off the charging port.

MIL-STD-810 is the US military standard for environmental testing. It is a collection of test methods, and vibration is Method 514. Our MIL-STD-810H guide covers the drop test. Method 514 sets out how to shake equipment to represent what it will meet in service, and two of its procedures matter for a tablet. In general vibration, the device is fixed to a shaker table and run through a profile matched to a type of vehicle, for a set time on each axis, then checked for function. In loose cargo, it is left unsecured in a container shaken to simulate the back of a lorry.

The manufacturer picks the procedure and the profile, and a van dashboard and an excavator cab are very different tests, so "tested to Method 514" without the detail is a weak claim. And a MIL-STD-810 rating on its own does not tell you vibration was tested at all, only that the manufacturer declared compliance with the methods it chose.

That applies to our own range. The A6, A8, A10 and WH6 are listed to MIL-STD-810H and the W8 to MIL-STD-810G. Our listings give the rating, not the methods run, and we do not list vibration testing for any device, so do not assume it. The W10 has no MIL-STD rating listed. If vibration testing decides your purchase, ask us before you order.

Mounting on Plant and Machinery

A digger, dumper, tractor or forklift usually shakes harder than a van, and the cab has nowhere to put a tablet that is not in the way. So ask first whether it needs to be in the cab at all. If it is for checking drawings, logging hours and photographing the job, it can stay in the site cabin and come out only when needed, which takes the vibration problem away. A shelf or a bag in the cabin costs nothing; a dock in the cabin means a Charging Dock (£49.99).

If it must be mounted while the machine works:

  • Bolt to a proper machine bracket or an existing mounting point, never to a plastic panel and never with suction. Check with the machine's maker or hirer before drilling the cab structure.
  • Use the shortest arm you can, with some damping at the base.
  • Check the fixings each shift; bolts back out under vibration.
  • Expect dust, wet and temperature swings. The A8 and A10 are sealed to IP68 and list an operating range of -20°C to 60°C. The W8 is IP67 and -10°C to 50°C. The W10 is IP65 with no operating range listed. Our IP ratings guide explains the sealing grades.

Charging on the Move

A docked tablet is kept charged while you drive. Without a dock, the 12V DC Car Charger (£26) plugs into the vehicle's 12V socket.

Batteries differ a lot: 10600mAh in the A6, 10000mAh in the A8, 8000mAh in the A10, 5000mAh in the W8 and W10 and 4000mAh in the WH6. The Windows devices carry the smaller batteries (4000 to 5000mAh), so check how long a shift you need before relying on one without charging. The WH6 and W10 batteries are removable, so you can swap in a charged spare battery if you have one.

Two cautions. Do not charge a frozen device: a tablet left in the van overnight in winter needs to warm up first, and our winter battery guide explains why. And a dashboard in summer sun, with the tablet charging and the screen on full, is hard on any tablet battery, so if it is not needed on the dash, put it in the pocket.

Pogo Pin Docks, Explained

Pogo pins are spring loaded contacts in a dock that press onto contact pads on the device. Drop the device in and it charges, with no plug, no cable and nothing to line up in the dark.

The advantage over USB-C is wear. A plug does not like sideways load, which is exactly what a cable gets in a moving van. Pogo pads have nothing to insert, so docking puts no sideways load on a port.

In our range, the A10, WH6, W8 and W10 all list a pogo pin connector. The Charging Dock (£49.99) is a desktop dock with a pogo pin connection, listed as fitting the A8, A10, W8 and W10, and the WH6 Charging Cradle (£79) does the same job for the WH6. The A8 is listed as fitting the dock but does not list a pogo pin connector itself, so ask us how it docks before you order.

The Vehicle Mounting Dock is listed with a DC power input; its listing does not say how it connects to the tablet, so ask us if port wear matters to you.

Using It While Driving

A mounted tablet is a route screen and a job list, not something to operate on the move. Set the route and open the job before you pull away, and deal with messages and new jobs when you are stopped. Rules on using a device at the wheel are set out in the Highway Code and on GOV.UK, so read the current version for yourself. The safe habit is simpler than any rule: hands off the screen while the vehicle is moving.
