What Work at Height Does to a Device

Roofing and scaffolding share a problem most trades do not have: the device is rarely on a flat surface. It is on a pitched roof, a scaffold board or a ladder, and the person holding it has one hand on something else. A tablet that slides off a roof does not fall a metre onto a kitchen floor. It falls a storey or more onto concrete, or onto whoever is below.

So the two things that decide whether a device survives this trade are whether it stays attached to you, and whether you can read the screen with the sun behind you. The drop rating comes third, because a figure tested at 1.5 metres says nothing about a fall from a gable end. The strap matters more than the number.

A word on safety: this guide is about the device. The rules and training for working at height are between you, your employer and the relevant guidance, and nothing here changes them.

Four Things to Weigh Up

1. Can You Read It in Full Sun?

Roofs are the brightest place most tradespeople work: no shade, a reflective surface, and the sun overhead or behind you. Brightness is measured in nits, and our guide to screen brightness explains what the figures mean outdoors. In our range the A8 lists 1000 nits, the W8 550, the WH6 400 and the A10 350. No figure is listed for the A6 or the W10, so do not assume either is especially bright. For a device that spends its day on a roof, the A8 is the obvious choice on this measure alone.

2. Can You Hold It With One Hand?

The A6 is 277g and 6 inches, which is a large phone. The A10 is 680g at 10.1 inches, fine in two hands or on a knee but tiring held up with one. The W8 is 910g and the W10 is 1197g. No weight is listed for the A8. If the device goes up the ladder with you, think handheld or 8 inch. If it mostly stays at ground level and comes up for a drawing or a photo, the 10 inch screen is worth the weight.

3. What Is the Drop Rating, Honestly?

Drop specs on our devices are what the manufacturer lists and nothing more. The A6 is rated for 1.5 metre drops. The WH6 is rated for 1.5 metre drops and tested to MIL-STD-810H. The A10 is tested to MIL-STD-810H, with no metre figure listed. The W8 is tested to MIL-STD-810G, the older revision, with 1.22 metre drops. The A8 and the W10 list no drop rating at all, which means no figure is published, so we do not claim one.

Our MIL-STD-810H guide explains what the standard covers. Every one of these figures is a waist-height drop onto a hard surface. None is a fall from a roof. Treat the drop rating as insurance against the everyday fumble, and the strap as what keeps the device out of the skip.

4. How Does It Stay Attached?

Three things on our accessories page are built to keep a device on your body rather than in your hand.

  • The Hand Strap (£24), adjustable with 360 degree rotation, fits the A8, A10, W8 and W10 for one-handed use on a ladder or a board. The A6, A10, W8 and W10 come with a hand strap in the box; the A8 does not, so budget for one.
  • The Shoulder Strap (£19), padded with quick release clips, fits the A8, A10, W8 and W10 and keeps the tablet hanging from you rather than resting on a sloping surface when you need both hands.
  • The Belt Holster (£22) fits the A6 and the WH6. It is MOLLE compatible with a magnetic closure, and keeps a handheld on your belt rather than in a pocket it can fall out of when you bend over a verge.

Whatever you pick, clip the device on before you climb. A strap in the van does nothing.
