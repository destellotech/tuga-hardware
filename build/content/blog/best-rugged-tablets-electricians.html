Start With the Software, Not the Spec Sheet

A rugged tablet earns its place by running the software you already use, in the places you already use it. So before comparing IP ratings, write down what it has to run:

  • Certificate software. Digital certificate software exists. Whether your package runs on the device is a question for its provider, so check before you buy.
  • Test instrument apps. Some multifunction testers pair with a phone or tablet over Bluetooth and send readings to a companion app. Every device in our range lists Bluetooth, but check the app exists for the operating system you are buying and that your tester supports it.
  • Job management and quoting. Diary, quotes, invoices and customer sign-off. Check whether yours is browser based or has an Android app.
  • Drawings and manuals. A PDF reader for layouts and schedules, and a browser for product data sheets.
  • Photos and messaging. The camera, cloud storage and whatever you use to send photos to the office.

If everything on the list runs on Android or in a browser, buy Android. If one program you cannot do without is Windows only, buy Windows and accept the higher price. Our guide to Android vs Windows rugged tablets walks through that decision in detail. Either way, install your software in the first few days and run a test job through it at your desk, so any surprise shows up while the returns window is open.

Entering Test Results Without Taking Your Gloves Off

An inspection day is hours of entering readings, circuit by circuit. Three things make that quicker.

Glove mode. The A8 lists glove mode. The A6, the Tuga A10 and the Windows models do not list it. Gloves vary, so try yours when the device arrives. The stylus below helps when the gloves come off.

A stylus. The Capacitive Stylus at £14.99 is listed for the A8, A10, WH6, W8 and W10, but not the A6. On a screen full of small boxes it is more accurate than a gloved finger, and it makes a customer signature look like a signature.

Screen size. A schedule of test results has a lot of columns. Forms vary, but the larger the screen, the less sideways scrolling. The A6 has 6 inches, the A8 has 8 and the A10 has 10.1 inches at Full HD (1920 x 1200). Size costs weight: the A6 is 277g, the A8 and A10 are both 680g. All three come with a hand strap in the box, which matters when you are holding the tablet one handed at the top of a ladder.

Photographing Installs and Dark Cupboards

Photos are part of the paperwork now: the board before and after, cable routes, the earthing arrangement, the meter, the fault you found. Two things matter here, and neither is megapixels.

Light. Consumer units live in cupboards under stairs, and cable runs live in lofts. All three list a 13MP rear camera. The A6 and A10 list a flash with it; the A8 does not. For a well lit board any of them is fine. For a loft or a void, a flash helps, and a head torch helps more.

Storage and upload. Photos add up over a year. The A8 has 128GB with a microSD slot that takes cards up to 512GB. The A6 has 64GB with microSD up to 256GB, and the A10 has 128GB with microSD up to 2TB. For uploads, all three have 4G LTE, and the A10 and A6 are dual SIM, so they can carry a second network where the first is patchy. On the A6 the second SIM shares its slot with the microSD card.

Lofts, Drawings and Daylight

A loft is the opposite of a sunny roof: cramped, dark and hot in summer. What matters is readability at arm's length, one handed use, and a body rated for the temperature. The A8 and A10 are listed for -20°C to 60°C operation and the A6 for -20°C to 50°C, so check that range against the conditions you work in. In winter, batteries still lose capacity in the cold, and our cold weather battery guide covers what to expect.

For a domestic rewire layout or a lighting plan, 8 inches is enough. For a commercial distribution schematic or a full floor plan with containment runs, the A10's Full HD screen saves a lot of pinching and zooming. The trade-off is outdoors: the A10 is rated at 350 nits and the A8 at 800, so if part of the day is spent at external meter boxes or plant on a roof, the A8 is far easier to read in sunlight. No brightness figure is listed for the A6.

Dust, Damp and Drops

Chasing walls, drilling joists and opening up old consumer units throw out fine dust that goes straight into charging ports and speaker grilles. The A8 and A10 are sealed to IP68, which means dust tight and rated for immersion, and both are tested to MIL-STD-810H. The A6 is sealed to IP67, also dust tight and rated for immersion, and lists a 1.5 metre drop rating rather than a MIL-STD rating. None of them is rated for pressure washing. Our MIL-STD-810H guide explains what the rating does and does not tell you. The short version: it is a set of defined test methods, not a pass mark, and the rating on its own does not tell you which tests a given device went through. Ask before you assume more.

The Windows models are sealed differently. The W8 is IP67 and lists MIL-STD-810G. The WH6 and W10 are IP65, which covers dust and water jets but not immersion. For most electrical work that is enough, since the enemy is dust and damp rather than a bucket, but it is a point in Android's favour on wet sites.

Battery: An Inspection Day Is a Long Day

The A8 has a 10000mAh battery and the A10 has 8000mAh. The A6 has 5050mAh, about half the A8's, but it is removable. With the screen on for most of a testing day you want the bigger figure, and a 12V DC Car Charger at £26, listed for all six devices, tops up between jobs. On the Windows side the W8 has 5000mAh, while the WH6 (4000mAh) and W10 (5000mAh) have removable batteries like the A6, so you can swap in a spare battery. We do not currently list a spare battery as an accessory.

When Windows Is the Right Call

If your certificate software, your design package or the office system is Windows only, you need a Windows device, and the sensible one for an electrician is the Tuga W8 at £629: an 8 inch Windows 11 Pro tablet with a 700 nit screen, IP67 sealing, a fingerprint sensor, and USB-A and HDMI alongside USB-C. It is £250 more than the A8, its battery is half the size at 5000mAh, and its listed operating range is -10°C to 50°C rather than -20°C to 60°C. It is a purchase you make because the software requires it, not for the hardware.

If you also do data installs and want to plug straight into a switch or a panel, the Tuga W10 at £649 lists an RJ45 Ethernet port, though at 1197g and 10.1 inches it is a different thing to carry up a ladder. Before committing, ask your software provider whether an Android or browser version exists or is coming. It may save you the premium.
