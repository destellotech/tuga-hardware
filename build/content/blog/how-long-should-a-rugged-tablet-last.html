How long a rugged tablet lasts depends less on the tough body than on the battery, the software support and how the seals and ports are treated. The body is built to take knocks. The battery loses capacity with every charge, and the software stops getting updates at some point. So plan for both: check how the battery can be replaced and how long the software will be supported before you buy.

What Ends a Rugged Tablet's Working Life

Rugged tablets get retired for one of these reasons:

  • The battery no longer lasts a shift. Every rechargeable battery loses capacity with age and use. The device still works, but it needs charging at lunchtime, then mid morning.
  • The software falls out of support. Updates stop, and eventually an app you rely on needs a newer version than the device can run.
  • A port wears out. The charging port is plugged and unplugged every day for years, often with grit in it.
  • The screen takes one hit too many. Rugged glass is built to take knocks, but a sharp corner at the wrong angle can still crack it.
  • The seals age. The gaskets that earned the IP rating harden over time. The tablet still works, but it is no longer as waterproof as it was on day one.

Only the last two are about the rugged part of a rugged tablet. The first three wear out on any device, rugged or not, and that changes what you should look at when you buy.

Battery Wear

Rechargeable batteries wear in two ways. Each charge and discharge cycle takes a little capacity away permanently, and the battery also ages while it sits, faster when it is hot. No rugged device is exempt. What differs is how much it matters:

  • A bigger battery cycles less deeply. If a large battery only drops to half by the end of a shift, it is not completing a full cycle a day, and it will outlast a small one that is run flat daily.   • A removable battery makes a worn cell easier to deal with. If the user can swap the battery, a worn one is a part, not a reason to replace the tablet. Keep the tablet out of direct sun and hot cabs while it charges, top it up rather than running it flat, and never charge a frozen device; our winter battery guide explains why that damage is permanent.

Software Support: Android and Windows Age Differently

A tablet does not stop working on the day its updates stop, but a gap opens between what the device runs and what your apps expect, and sooner or later something you rely on asks for a version it cannot give.

Android. Operating system and security patch updates for a given model come from its manufacturer, not automatically from Google. Each manufacturer decides how many years of operating system updates and security patches a model gets, and the period varies a lot from one model to the next. Once updates stop, you are on borrowed time with anything that demands a current version of Android.

Our Android devices do not all start in the same place. The A6 ships with Android 15, the A10 with Android 14 and the A8 with Android 12. That makes the A8's operating system three versions older than the A6's on the day you buy it, so it starts nearer the point where apps begin asking for something newer. If a long software life is a priority, that counts against the A8: ask us about its update support before you order, and check that the apps you rely on support Android 12.

Windows. Windows updates come from Microsoft, which publishes a support end date for each edition and version. Firmware and driver updates are the device maker's job, so ask about those too. Microsoft's published lifecycle dates can make a Windows tablet easier to plan around, but the dates depend on the edition: the WH6 runs Windows 11 IoT and the W8 and W10 run Windows 11 Pro, so look up the lifecycle for the one you are buying.

We do not list update periods for our devices on the product pages. If support length matters to you, ask us about the specific model before you order. Also check that the apps you depend on run on the device you are considering, rather than assuming they will.

Seals Age, So Treat the IP Rating as a Day One Figure

An IP68 rating tells you what the device survived when it was new. The rubber gaskets around the screen, buttons and any port covers keep the water out, and heat, sunlight, cleaning chemicals, oils and solvents all harden rubber over time. Repeated drops can also disturb a gasket, and a gasket that has moved can let water in. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide covers what the ratings mean.

That does not make the rating meaningless. It means a three year old device that has been hosed down every week should not be trusted in a bucket the way it was on day one. Practical steps:

  • If your device has port covers, close them fully after use. If a cover tears or no longer sits flush, do not trust the device in water until it has been checked; contact us if you are unsure.
  • Avoid solvents, which can attack rubber and plastic, and follow any cleaning guidance supplied with the device. Rinse with fresh water after salt spray or chemical splashes.
  • Do not pressure wash any device in our range. None carries an IP69K rating: the A10 is IP68, the A6 and A8 IP67, and the WH6, W8 and W10 IP65, and none of those covers high pressure, high temperature jets.

Heat matters here too. Do not leave the tablet baking on a dashboard or windscreen mount in summer sun.

Buying for a Long Life

If you want a tablet that lasts, these matter more than the drop height:

  • Software support length. Ask before you buy. It decides how long the device stays useful once the hardware is sound, and you cannot add it later. The version a device ships with is part of this: a newer starting point, such as Android 15 on the A6, leaves more road ahead than Android 12 on the A8.
  • A removable battery if you plan to keep the device past the point where the original cell is tired. In our range, the A6, A8, WH6, W8 and W10.
  • Memory and storage headroom. Apps grow and photo archives fill up. The A10, WH6, W8 and W10 have 8GB of RAM and 128GB of storage, and the A8 has 6GB and 128GB. The A6 handheld has 4GB and 64GB, so if you keep years of photos on the device, plan on a microSD card or cloud storage. The A6, A8, A10, WH6, W8 and W10 take a microSD card.

One caveat: if your work changes every couple of years, or your software supplier only supports the newest operating systems, paying for a long life you will not use may not be worth it. The cheapest rugged device that meets your spec today, replaced when it falls out of support, can be the better value. Our cost comparison uses a three year window to compare running costs. That is a comparison period, not a lifespan we can promise, so plan around the battery and the software support rather than a number of years.
