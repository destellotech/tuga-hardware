What Plumbing and Heating Work Does to a Device

This trade is hard on electronics in ordinary ways. Your hands are wet for much of the day. The device goes down on a tiled floor, a worktop beside a running tap, or the bottom of a bath you are fitting. It picks up flux, jointing compound and boiler dust, and lives under sinks, in airing cupboards and lofts, and on a van dashboard in January.

A consumer phone in a case can handle the paperwork: commissioning checklists, service records, landlord certificates, photos, a quote at the kitchen table and a signature before you go. It is the water, the drops and the dark cupboards that finish it off. So the shortlist comes down to sealing, a camera that works in poor light, a battery that lasts a day of callouts, and an operating system that runs your software. Start with the software.

Three Questions Before You Look at Specs

1. Does Any of Your Software Need Windows?

Boiler manufacturers can offer two kinds of software. The first is a phone app for commissioning, fault codes and service reminders, which may talk to the boiler over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. Apps like that are often written for phones. Check with the manufacturer that the app supports the Android version on the device (Android 15 on the A6, Android 14 on the A8), then try it on the device before you rely on it.

The second kind is diagnostic or service software that installs on a Windows PC and connects to the boiler through an interface lead. If your work involves that, you need a Windows device. Check with the manufacturer which platforms the software supports, whether it needs a specific interface, and whether it runs on Windows 11. Do not take a retailer's word for it, including ours.

If your flue gas analyser has a companion app for logging readings and producing the customer's report, check the analyser manufacturer's list of supported devices and Android versions. If you print to a separate printer, check that pairs too.

Our Android or Windows guide has a five minute test for this. One Windows only program you cannot do without means Windows. Everything else means Android, which is cheaper, lighter and, on the tablets, has bigger batteries.

2. How Wet Does It Get?

IP67 and IP68 are dust tight and rated for immersion, a metre for 30 minutes on IP67 and deeper on IP68. IP65 is dust tight and protected against water jets, but not immersion. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the ratings in full. For this trade: IP67 and IP68 are rated for immersion, so a drop into a bath is within the rating. IP65 is not rated for immersion.

One more rating matters here. IP69K covers high pressure, high temperature water jets, which is what lets a device survive jet washing. No device in our range carries it, so wipe them clean rather than hosing them down.

3. Handheld or Tablet?

Then decide between a handheld and a tablet. A handheld lives in a pocket or holster and comes out many times a day for a photo or a fault code. A tablet lives in the van and comes into the house for a quote or a long form. Some firms run both.

Winter Callouts
