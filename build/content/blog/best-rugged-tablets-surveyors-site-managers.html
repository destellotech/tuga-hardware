For a surveyor or site manager who reads drawings for a living, the Tuga A10 at £449 is the one to start with: a 10.1 inch 1920 x 1200 screen, IP68 sealing, MIL-STD-810H testing and 680g, which is the weight that matters at the end of a site walk. If a program you rely on only runs on Windows, the Tuga W10 at £649 brings Windows 11 Pro, Ethernet and HDMI, at 1197g. If your day is mostly snagging and photographs with the drawing as a reference, the 8 inch Tuga A8 at £379 is brighter outdoors and £70 cheaper than the A10.

Our surveyors and site managers page runs through the specs. This guide works the other way round: it takes the four jobs a tablet does on a survey or a site walk, drawings, snagging, mark-ups and the walk itself, and asks what each one needs from the device.

The Drawing Set

Start here, because the drawing set is what most people buy a 10 inch tablet for. A general arrangement at A1, a services layout with four disciplines overlaid. On a tablet you get one sheet at a time, so the screen has to show enough of it that you can find the grid line you want without zooming in and losing your place.

Two numbers decide that: physical size and resolution. The A10 and the W10 are both 10.1 inches, but the A10 shows 1920 x 1200 pixels and the W10 1280 x 800. The W10's resolution is the same as the 8 inch A8's, spread over a larger screen. For a dense drawing that means the A10 resolves fine text, dimension strings and hatching at a zoom level where the W10 is starting to blur them. Our 8 inch or 10 inch guide covers the size question in general; for drawing sets specifically, resolution counts as much as inches.

Then there is the size of the set. A full set of PDF drawings with revisions kept, plus the site photos beside it, adds up. Every device here starts at 128GB of storage. The A10 lists a microSD slot taking cards up to 2TB, and the W10 can be ordered with a 256GB storage upgrade for £69 or 512GB for £125, fitted before dispatch. The A8 lists a microSD slot without a stated limit. Our guide to how much storage a work tablet needs goes into what fills a device and when cloud sync is the better answer.

We cannot tell you how any particular viewer performs on these devices. Load your own heaviest sheet during the returns period and see.

The Site Walk

A site walk is the part the spec sheet undersells. You carry the tablet for hours, in one hand while the other holds a tape or a handrail, up scaffold stairs and across hardcore, often in weather. Weight, grip and sunlight decide whether it is a help or a burden.

On weight, the gap is large. The A10 is 680g. The W10 is 1197g, which is 517g more. The A8 does not list a weight, so we will not guess one. The A10 and W10 come with a hand strap in the box; the A8 does not, and the £24 Hand Strap on the accessories page fits it. A Shoulder Strap at £19 fits all three tablets and gets the device off your hands between stops.

On brightness, the ranking flips. The A8 is rated at 1000 nits, the A10 at 350 nits, and the W10 lists no figure. Reading a drawing in full sun on a 350 nit screen means shading it with your body or stepping into the lee of a cabin. If most of your walks are outdoors in summer, that is a real point for the A8. Our guide to screen brightness explains the numbers.

On weather and knocks: the A10 is sealed to IP68 and tested to MIL-STD-810H. The A8 is IP67 and lists no drop rating. The W10 is IP65, which covers dust and water jets but not immersion, and lists no MIL-STD rating. The MIL-STD-810H guide is honest about what the standard does and does not cover.

Snagging

Snagging is a camera job with a location attached: find the defect, photograph it, pin it to the drawing, note who owns it, check later that it is closed. The device needs a camera that copes with an unlit stairwell, enough screen to place the pin accurately, and a way to get the list off site the same day.

The A10 and A8 both have a 13MP rear camera with a flash, and the A8's is autofocus. The W10 has a 5MP autofocus rear camera and no flash is listed, which is adequate for a labelled defect in good light and weaker in a dark riser. If snagging is the bulk of your day, that alone can tip the choice to Android. Our guide to taking site photos that hold up covers framing, light and backing up.

Placing the pin is where the A10's resolution earns its keep again. At 1920 x 1200 you can see the whole floor plate and still read the room numbers, so the pin goes in the right room first time.

Getting the list off site needs a connection. The A10 and A8 have dual SIM 4G built in. On the W10, 4G is an option rather than standard.

Mark-ups and Sign-off

Mark-ups are where a stylus pays for itself. Red-lining with a finger is fine for a circle and an arrow and poor for a note in a tight corner. The £14.99 Capacitive Stylus is listed for the A8, A10 and W10. It is a passive stylus, a finer finger rather than a drawing pen, and for site mark-ups that is usually enough.

Sign-off is a form and a signature, and any of these devices handles that. The question is the software behind it. If your diary, document control, CAD viewer or survey processing software is Windows only, you are into W10 territory, and the Android or Windows guide has a five minute test to settle it. Do not take our word that an app runs well on a given tablet. Install it and try it.

Back in the Cabin

The site cabin is where the W10 makes its case. RJ45 Ethernet for the wired network, HDMI for a monitor, USB-A and USB-C for a keyboard or a memory stick from the main contractor. In the £49.99 Charging Dock with a screen attached, it is the office PC for the afternoon. Our guide to when a work tablet needs real ports goes through who actually plugs into what.

The Android tablets have USB-C only, and the £8 USB-C to USB-A OTG Adapter connects a memory stick or a keyboard. There is no HDMI or Ethernet on the A10 or A8, which is fine for most site managers and a daily irritation for the one who runs the progress meeting from the tablet.

It runs Android 12, older than the A10's Android 14, so check that your snagging and diary apps still support it before you buy.

Choosing Between Them

  1. Does any program you cannot do without run only on Windows? Yes means the W10. No means Android, and a cheaper, lighter device.
  2. Is the drawing the main event or the reference? Main event means the A10 for its resolution. Reference means the A8 is worth a look.
  3. How far do you walk, and how much of it is in the sun? Long walks favour the 680g A10. Long sunny days favour the 1000 nit A8.
  4. Who else needs the drawing on a screen? If you run meetings from the tablet, the W10's HDMI is worth its weight.
