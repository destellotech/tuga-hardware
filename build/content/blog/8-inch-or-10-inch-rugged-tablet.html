If you spend most of the day reading drawings, plans or wide spreadsheets on Android, buy the 10.1 inch Tuga A10, which has a 1920 x 1200 screen. If the tablet spends most of its day in one hand, up a ladder, in a loft or going in and out of a van door pocket, buy the 8 inch Tuga A8, which is also £70 cheaper. On Windows the W10 is bigger but has fewer pixels than the 8 inch W8 (1280 x 800 against 1920 x 1200), so the extra size gets you a bigger screen, not a sharper one, and adds 287g. The rest of this guide explains what a bigger screen gives you, what it costs you in brightness and weight, and the few figures that settle it.

In our range the choice is the Tuga A8 or Tuga A10 on Android, and the Tuga W8 or Tuga W10 on Windows 11. Decide the operating system first, because that choice is about your software and a different screen size will not fix it. Our guide to Android or Windows for trade work covers it. This guide assumes you have chosen, and covers the size decision.

What a 10.1 Inch Screen Actually Gives You

All four tablets have a 16:10 resolution, either 1280 x 800 or 1920 x 1200, so the screen shapes match. A 10.1 inch screen has roughly 60 per cent more area than an 8 inch one of the same shape. That is a lot more glass. But area is only half of the story. The other half is resolution.

Two of the four tablets have a 1280 x 800 screen, the A8 and the W10, and two have 1920 x 1200, the A10 and the W8. That is more than twice as many pixels. That changes what "bigger screen" means on each side of the range.

On Android, going from the A8 to the A10 gives you more area and more detail. A drawing viewed at a zoom where the text is legible shows noticeably more of the sheet on the A10, because it has both more area and more pixels to draw it with.

On Windows, going from the W8 to the W10 gives you more area but fewer pixels. The W8 has 1920 x 1200 and the W10 has 1280 x 800, so the 8 inch W8 shows more of a drawing at the same zoom, drawn smaller. On the W10, lines, text and buttons are physically bigger, which is easier to read at arm's length and easier to hit with a finger, but you fit less of the sheet on screen. If what you want from a Windows tablet is detail, that is worth knowing before you pay for the size.

Drawings, Plans and BIM Viewers

An A1 or A0 drawing on any tablet involves zooming. The question is how often. On the A10's 1920 x 1200 screen you can read a floor plan at a zoom that keeps a decent section of the sheet in view, then zoom into a detail and back out. On the 8 inch tablets you spend more of your time pinching, and a cluttered services drawing becomes a series of small windows. If reading drawings is the job, the A10 at £449 is the tablet our listing recommends for blueprints, BIM viewers and PDF plans, and it costs £70 more than the A8.

PDF plan viewers, BIM viewers and markup apps are all third party software. Check that the app you rely on runs on the device rather than assuming it will. If you carry drawing packs on the device, see how much storage a work tablet needs.

If you read drawings for a living, our guide for surveyors and site managers works through drawing sets, snagging and site walks device by device.

Spreadsheets and Long Forms

A spreadsheet is a column problem. What matters is how many columns fit before you scroll sideways, and that is governed by pixel width. The A10 has both the wider screen and the extra pixels, so more columns fit at a readable text size before you scroll sideways than on the A8. A long form, a schedule of test results or a materials list behaves the same way: fewer sideways scrolls.

On the W10, the extra size makes text at a given zoom easier to read, so you can zoom out a step and recover some columns that way. That is not the same as having the pixels, and the 8 inch W8 has more of them at 1920 x 1200. For a desktop spreadsheet or Windows job management program used at a desk or in a cab, check that your own software runs well on the device.

What the Bigger Screen Costs You

Weight and Size in the Hand

On Android, we cannot give you a like for like weight comparison, because no weight or dimensions are listed for the A8. The A10 weighs 680g and measures 250 x 167 x 14.5mm, which is larger than a sheet of A5 paper and well short of A4, and it is thin. If you were avoiding the A10 because you assumed a 10 inch tablet must be a lump, its listed weight says otherwise. If weight in the hand is what decides it for you, ask us through the contact form before you order, or weigh the A8 yourself when it arrives, within the 30 day returns window.

On Windows it is a different story. The W8 weighs 910g. The W10 weighs 1197g, which is 287g more and nearly a third heavier. It measures 280 x 198 x 22mm, not far off a sheet of A4 and 22mm thick. Carried around a site all day, you will feel it, and you will want the £19 Shoulder Strap, which is an accessory and not in the box. The W10 has a removable 5000mAh battery, RJ45 Ethernet and HDMI in the body, and the Vehicle Mounting Dock and Charging Dock are both listed as fitting it.

Brightness: the 8 Inch Android Wins Outdoors

The second difference, on the Android side, is how usable the screen is in daylight. The A8 is listed at 1000 nits, the highest brightness figure in our range. The A10 is 350 nits. That is a big gap. At 350 nits the A10 is the dimmer screen, so it suits a cab, a site office or work under cover better than open sun. On a bright roof or an open field in summer, the A8 is the one you can read without cupping a hand over it.

Neither the A8 nor the A10 lists a glove mode. If you work in gloves and do not want to pull one off for every tap, check how the screen responds with your own gloves as soon as it arrives.

On Windows, the W8 is listed at 550 nits, which is better than the A10 and WH6 but well short of the A8. We do not list a brightness figure for the W10, so we will not guess at one. If you will use the W10 outdoors, ask us through the contact form before you order, or check the screen in your own conditions as soon as it arrives, within the 30 day returns window.

One Hand or Two

The practical test is whether you can hold the tablet in one hand and work it with your thumb. An 8 inch tablet passes that test more easily. A 10.1 inch tablet does not, really. Size and weight are separate on Windows. The 8 inch W8 weighs 910g, which is 230g more than the 10.1 inch A10 at 680g, so choose the W8 for Windows software, not for lightness. You hold it like a clipboard, or it lives on a flat surface, a mount or a stand. The A10, W8 and W10 come with a hand strap in the box. The A8 does not, but the £24 Hand Strap on our accessories page is listed as fitting it.

The A8 listing is written around this. It says what the tablet is for: forms, maps, site photos and quotes, readable in full sun, on a tablet small enough to hold in one hand up a ladder or drop into a van door pocket. If that describes your day, the extra screen is not worth having, and the cheaper tablet is the better one.

Battery, Sealing and Ports Also Change with Size

The two sizes are not the same tablet with a different screen, so check the rest of the sheet before you decide.

  • Battery. The two Android tablets have the same capacity, 8000mAh, but the A8's battery is removable, so a charged spare can take over on a long day. The A10 lists quick charge; no charging speed is listed for the A8. The W8 and W10 both have 5000mAh, and both are removable.
  • Sealing and MIL-STD rating. The A10 is IP68 and MIL-STD-810H, rated for -20°C to 60°C. The A8 is IP67, with no MIL-STD rating, drop rating or operating temperature listed. The W8 is IP65 and MIL-STD-810G, rated for -20°C to 60°C with a 1.22m drop rating. The W10 is IP65, with no MIL-STD rating or operating temperature listed. If the tablet will see puddles and buckets, our guide to IP68 vs IP67 explains why that matters, and MIL-STD-810H explained covers what a MIL-STD rating does and does not tell you.
  • Connectivity. The A8 and A10 both have dual SIM 4G, useful where one network is patchy. The W8 has 4G LTE built in. The W10 has Wi-Fi and 100Mbps Ethernet as standard with 4G LTE as an option, so if it will work away from Wi-Fi, ask us through the contact form about the 4G LTE option before you order.
  • Ports. The A8 has USB-C, a DC input and pogo pin contacts, with VESA mount points on the back, but no USB-A. The A10 has USB-C, a 3.5mm headphone jack and a pogo pin connector. The W8 has USB-C, USB-A and Mini HDMI. The W10 has HDMI and adds RJ45 Ethernet to that list, for direct connections to plant, panels and network gear that has never seen Wi-Fi.
