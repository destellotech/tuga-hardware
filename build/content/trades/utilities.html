Water, gas, power and telecoms engineers carry software most trades never see. Diagnostic and commissioning tools, protection relay configurators, network analysers, GIS viewers and asset databases, and a lot of it only runs on Windows with a cable plugged in. The tablet has to run that, and still survive a substation yard in the rain.

What kills devices in utilities work

  • Weather with no cover. Pumping stations, pole tops, cabinets on the verge and open compounds. Rain blows sideways and there is no van door to shut.
  • Chambers and cabinets. Wet, cramped and dark. The device goes on a ledge or the floor of the chamber while both hands are busy.
  • Cables and adapters. A laptop with a dongle hanging off it is one knock away from a snapped port. Each adapter is another thing to lose.
  • Cold starts. A device left in the vehicle overnight in winter has to work first thing, and not every spec sheet promises it will.
  • Long shifts with no socket. Commissioning runs long, and the battery has to last or be swappable.

What actually matters in a spec

Ports. If your software talks to equipment over a cable, you want the port on the tablet, not on an adapter. The W10 lists 100Mbps RJ45 Ethernet, HDMI, USB-A and USB-C. The WH6 lists USB-A and USB-C. Android tablets have USB-C and need an adapter for anything else.

The operating system is decided for you. If the tools you use every day are Windows only, that is the answer. If your work is mostly work orders, inspections and photos in an Android app, you can spend less. Our guide to Android or Windows for a rugged tablet has a quick test for deciding.

What IP65 actually means. IP65 means dust tight and protected against water jets from any direction. It does not mean the device can be dropped in a flooded chamber. IP67 and IP68 cover immersion. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the difference. None of our devices is rated IP69K, so do not pressure wash any of them.

Drop and temperature ratings, where they exist. MIL-STD-810H tells you a device has been through a set of published tests. Read what was tested, because the standard is a method, not a certificate. Our MIL-STD-810H guide goes through it. Where a device lists no rating at all, treat that as unknown, not as a pass.

A removable battery. A spare in the bag beats a power bank hanging off the port when a commissioning job overruns.

Connectivity. Rural substations and treatment works often have poor coverage. Built-in 4G with dual SIM helps where one network is weak. Our connectivity guide covers when a tablet needs its own SIM.

Will my commissioning software run on it?

We cannot answer that for your software. The W10 and WH6 run full Windows 11 on Intel processors with 8GB of RAM, so standard Windows desktop applications install as they would on a laptop, but vendor drivers, USB interface cables and licensing dongles are where things go wrong. Buy one unit, install your tools, plug in your cables, and test on a real job before ordering for the team.

Can I use it in the rain?

Yes, within the rating. IP65 on the W10 and WH6 covers rain and water jets. IP68 on the A10 also covers immersion. None of them is rated for pressure washing.
