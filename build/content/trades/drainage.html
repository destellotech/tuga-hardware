Drainage is one of the few trades where the device is wet for most of the day on purpose. Jetting, unblocking, lifting covers and lowering a camera into a chamber all happen with wet hands, in spray, often in rain.

What kills devices in drainage work

  • Spray and splashes. Jetting throws water everywhere. Rinsing a camera head, coiling a hose or opening a chamber in the rain puts water on the device several times an hour.
  • Drops into the wet. Onto a wet pavement, into a gully, or the slow slide off a manhole cover into the chamber below.
  • Grit, silt and effluent. It is on your gloves, so it is on the screen and in the port. A device that cannot be rinsed under a tap is a problem in this trade.
  • Survey software that wants Windows. Many CCTV survey and reporting packages were written for a Windows laptop, and some connect to the camera unit over a cable. That one fact decides which device you can use.

What actually matters in a spec

Immersion, not just splashes. Look for IP67 or IP68. Both mean dust tight and rated for immersion: IP67 for a metre of water for 30 minutes, IP68 for deeper and longer. IP65 means dust tight and protected against water jets, but not rated for immersion, so a device that goes into a flooded chamber is outside its rating. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the ratings in full. One more: none of our devices is rated IP69K, the rating for high pressure, high temperature jets, so clean them under a tap or with a cloth, never with the jetter.

A camera that works in the dark. Photos of a chamber, a collapsed section or a root mass settle disputes with customers and insurers. A rear camera with a flash matters more than its megapixel count when you are pointing it down a hole.

Windows or Android. If your CCTV survey or reporting software only runs on Windows, you need a Windows device, and you need to check with the software vendor which Windows version it supports and how it connects to the camera unit. If your work is job sheets, photos, quotes and a mapping app, Android is cheaper and the tablets have bigger batteries. Our Android or Windows guide has a five minute test, and can a rugged tablet replace a laptop covers what a Windows tablet can and cannot take over from the laptop in the van.

Ports, if the camera unit needs them. Some survey kit talks to the computer over USB or HDMI. When a work tablet needs real ports lists which of our devices has which socket.

A battery for a long day. A removable battery means a charged spare takes over at the van rather than the device going on charge mid job.

Questions drainage engineers ask

Can I rinse it off after a job?

The A8 (IP67) and A10 (IP68) are rated for immersion, so a rinse under a tap with the ports closed is well within what they are built for. The W8 is IP65, so wipe it down rather than rinsing it. None of them is rated IP69K, so keep every device away from the jetter and the pressure washer.

Will my CCTV survey software run on it?

Only the vendor can tell you. Ask them which operating systems and versions they support, and how the software connects to the camera unit. If the answer is Windows, the W8 runs Windows 11 Pro and has USB-A and Mini HDMI. If the answer is Android or a web browser, the A8 and A10 are cheaper and sealed for the wet. Trial it on one device before you buy for the team.

Can I use the screen with wet hands or gloves?

None of the three lists a glove mode or a wet touch mode, so expect to dry your hands or take a glove off for fine work. A stylus helps.
