For surveyors and site managers the tablet is the drawing, the camera and the notebook at once, and it is used a long way from the site office. The two things that decide the purchase are screen size and weight, and they pull in opposite directions.

What kills devices on survey and site work

  • Weather. A survey does not stop for rain. The device is out in it for hours, not just for a dash between the van and the cabin.
  • Dust and mud. Cutting dust, cement and wet clay get into ports and under screen edges, and a muddy glove across the glass scratches it.
  • Drops onto hard ground. Off a scaffold lift, out of a cab, off a wall you were measuring from. Concrete and hardcore do not forgive.
  • Being carried all day. A tablet in one hand and an instrument in the other for several hours is where weight and a strap stop being details.
  • Glare. Reading a drawing in full sun is where a dim screen fails you.

What actually matters in a spec

Screen size and resolution. An A1 drawing on an 8 inch screen is a lot of pinching and zooming. The A10 has a 10.1 inch screen at 1920 x 1200, which is the difference between reading a plan and hunting around it. The W10 is also 10.1 inches but at 1280 x 800, the same resolution as the 8 inch A8. Our 8 inch or 10 inch guide goes through the trade off.

Weight on a long walk. The A10 weighs 680g and the W10 1197g, a difference of 517g that you will notice by the end of a site walk. The A8 does not list a weight. A shoulder strap or hand strap is worth having on any of them.

Sealing and drop testing. The A10 is sealed to IP68 and tested to MIL-STD-810H. The A8 is IP67 and lists no drop rating. The W10 is IP65, which covers dust and water jets but not immersion, and lists no MIL-STD rating. Our MIL-STD-810H guide explains what the standard does and does not tell you.

Brightness. The A8 is rated at 1000 nits, the A10 at 350 nits, and the W10 lists no brightness figure. If most of your day is outside in summer, that is a real point in the A8's favour.

Location. Measuring and mapping apps lean on the device's positioning. The A10 lists GPS, Galileo, BeiDou, QZSS and NavIC, the A8 lists GPS, GLONASS, Galileo and BeiDou, and on the W10 GPS is an option rather than standard. Check what accuracy your app expects from a tablet's own receiver.

Your software. Drawing viewers, snagging tools, PDF mark-up and most measuring apps run on Android or in a browser. Survey and CAD software that only runs on Windows forces the W10, which also brings Ethernet and HDMI for the site office. Check your own software on the device before buying a fleet.

Questions surveyors ask

8 inch or 10 inch?

If you read drawings for a living, 10 inch, and the A10's Full HD resolution is the reason to pick it. If you mostly photograph, snag and sign off, 8 inch, which is brighter outdoors and £70 cheaper.

Do I need Windows?

Only if a program you cannot do without is Windows only. Viewing PDFs, marking up drawings and most measuring and mapping apps run on Android. CAD authoring belongs on a laptop; if you need it on site, the W10 is the one to look at. Our Android or Windows guide has a five minute test.
