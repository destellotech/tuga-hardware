Consumer tablets rarely die of anything dramatic on electrical jobs. They die slowly, of the ordinary conditions of the work, and then quickly, of a drop onto a tiled floor.

What kills devices in electrical work

  • Plaster and brick dust. Chasing walls and opening up consumer units fills the air with fine dust that works its way into charging ports, speaker grilles and buttons.
  • Damp meter cupboards and outbuildings. Condensation, garages, external meter boxes and the odd leaking pipe. Enough moisture, often enough, to finish off a phone in a soft case.
  • Lofts, voids and under floors. Dark and cramped. Things get balanced on joists and then fall off them.
  • Steps and ladders. A tablet dropped from the top of a stepladder onto a hard floor is the single most common way a consumer screen breaks.

What actually matters in a spec

Sealing first. Look for IP68 rather than "splash resistant". The first digit covers dust, and a 6 means dust tight, which is the bit that matters in a dusty consumer unit. If you want the difference between IP67 and IP68 explained properly, our IP68 vs IP67 guide covers it.

Drop testing. MIL-STD-810H is a set of test methods rather than a promise, but it means the device is designed to take drops that would crack a normal tablet.

A screen you can use with gloves on. Glove mode saves taking gloves off every time you tap in a reading.

Battery that lasts a long day. EICR days can mean hours of entering circuit after circuit.

Android is fine. Most EICR certification apps and job management apps run on Android or in a browser. Unless your office insists on a piece of Windows desktop software, there is no reason to pay noticeably more for a Windows device.

Questions electricians ask

Will my certification app run on it?

If the app is available for Android or runs in a web browser, yes. If it is Windows only, you will need one of the Windows models. Check with your software provider, or ask us if you are unsure.

Can I use it with gloves on?

The A8 has glove mode. The A6 does not list glove mode, though its side scan keys work with gloves on; for the touchscreen you may need to take a glove off. Very thick insulated gloves will still be clumsy on any screen, which is where the stylus helps on the A8.

Is it worth it over a cheap tablet in a rugged case?

A case helps with drops but does nothing for dust in the ports or damp. We have written up the sums in rugged vs consumer tablet cost.
