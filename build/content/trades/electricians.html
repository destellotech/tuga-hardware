Consumer tablets rarely die of anything dramatic on electrical jobs. They die slowly, of the ordinary conditions of the work, and then quickly, of a drop onto a tiled floor.

What kills devices in electrical work

  • Plaster and brick dust. Chasing walls and opening up consumer units fills the air with fine dust that works its way into charging ports, speaker grilles and buttons.
  • Damp meter cupboards and outbuildings. Condensation, garages, external meter boxes and the odd leaking pipe. Enough moisture, often enough, to finish off a phone in a soft case.
  • Lofts, voids and under floors. Dark and cramped. Things get balanced on joists and then fall off them.
  • Steps and ladders. A tablet dropped from the top of a stepladder onto a hard floor is exactly the kind of fall that cracks a consumer screen.

What actually matters in a spec

Sealing first. Look for IP67 or IP68 rather than "splash resistant". The first digit covers dust, and a 6 means dust tight, which is the bit that matters in a dusty consumer unit. If you want the difference between IP67 and IP68 explained properly, our IP68 vs IP67 guide covers it.

Drop testing. A listed drop height or a MIL-STD-810H rating is a set of test conditions rather than a promise, but it means the device is designed to take drops that would crack a normal tablet. Check what each device actually lists: four of ours are tested to MIL-STD-810H, and the A6 and W10 carry their makers' own drop ratings instead.

A screen you can read where you are working. Outside on a bright day, a dim screen means squinting at every reading. Look for a brightness figure in nits, not just "sunlight readable".

Battery that lasts a long day. EICR days can mean hours of entering circuit after circuit. A removable battery means a charged spare can take over rather than you hunting for a socket.

Android is fine. Most EICR certification apps and job management apps run on Android or in a browser. Unless your office insists on a piece of Windows desktop software, there is no reason to pay noticeably more for a Windows device.

Questions electricians ask

Will my certification app run on it?

If the app runs in a web browser, or is available for Android and supports the version on the device, it should. The A8 runs Android 14 and the A6 Android 15, so check the minimum Android version your app asks for. If it is Windows only, you will need one of the Windows models. Check with your software provider, or ask us if you are unsure.

Can I use it with gloves on?

Neither the A8 nor the A6 lists a glove mode for the touchscreen, so try your own gloves on it, and expect to take a glove off at times. The A6's side scan keys work with gloves on. Very thick insulated gloves will be clumsy on any screen, which is where the stylus helps on the A8.

Is it worth it over a cheap tablet in a rugged case?

A case helps with drops but does nothing for dust in the ports or damp. We have written up the sums in rugged vs consumer tablet cost.
