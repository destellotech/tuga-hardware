A warehouse device is scanned with, not typed on. It books goods in, confirms picks and counts stock hundreds of times a shift, and it gets dropped, knocked against racking and shared between whoever is on. This page is about work inside the building. For drivers and deliveries, see our logistics and delivery page.

What kills devices in the warehouse

  • Drops onto concrete. From a forklift seat, off a pallet, off a trolley. The drops are daily, not occasional.
  • Cardboard dust and grit. Boxes shed dust that gets into ports and behind buttons. Loading bays add road grit and, in winter, salt.
  • Shared use and shift handover. A device used by three people a day is nobody's responsibility. It gets left on a shelf, dropped in a tote and put on charge wet.
  • Cold stores and chillers. Chilled areas drain batteries and fog screens on the way out, and not every rugged device lists a rating low enough for them.
  • Dead spots in the Wi-Fi. Steel racking and full pallets block signal, and a device that drops its connection in aisle 14 loses picks.

What actually matters in a spec

A real scan engine. Phone cameras read barcodes slowly and badly on shrink wrap, curled labels and in poor light. A dedicated 2D scan engine with a physical scan key reads at arm's length first time, all day. Whether you need one built in, or a separate Bluetooth scanner is enough, depends on how many scans a shift you do. Our guide to rugged tablets with a built-in barcode scanner compares the options.

Drop rating. Look for a listed drop height or a MIL-STD-810 rating, not just an IP rating. The A6 is rated for 1.5 metre drops, the WH6 for 1.5 metre drops and MIL-STD-810H, and the W8 is tested to MIL-STD-810G.

Size and weight. Pickers want a device that goes in a holster and leaves both hands free. Supervisors doing counts on a bigger screen may want a tablet. The A6 is 277g, the WH6 is 470g and the W8 is 750g.

Battery through a shift. A removable battery means a charged spare takes over at handover rather than the device going on charge mid shift. The A6 and WH6 have removable batteries; the W8 is not listed as having one.

Operating temperature. For chillers and cold stores, check the listed range. The A6 is rated from -20°C to 50°C, the WH6 from -20°C to 60°C and the W8 from -10°C to 50°C. The W10 lists no operating temperature, so we cannot recommend it for cold areas.

Your warehouse system decides the operating system. Most modern stock control and warehouse management apps run on Android or in a browser. Some older systems only have a Windows client, and if yours does, that settles it and adds £350 to £430 to the price of a scanner device. One honest alternative. If scanning is occasional, a few deliveries a day rather than hundreds of picks, a plain tablet paired with a separate Bluetooth scanner can be the cheaper answer, and the scanner can be shared. The built-in engines pay off when scanning is the job: one handed, a physical key, and no second device to charge or lose.

Does the scanner work with our stock system?

The A6 scan engine reads 1D barcodes and 2D codes such as QR and passes the result to whatever app is on screen. Whether your stock control app runs on Android 15, or needs the Windows client on the WH6 or W8, is something to check with your own software before you buy.
