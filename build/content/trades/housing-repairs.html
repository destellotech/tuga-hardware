A repairs operative's tablet is the job sheet, the camera, the map to the next address and the proof that the work was done. It is also, in most teams, a shared asset: picked up at the depot, carried through twenty homes a week and handed to whoever is on the next shift.

What kills devices in housing repairs work

  • Being shared. A device nobody owns gets dropped in footwells, left on van roofs and charged with whatever cable is to hand. Rugged housings and removable batteries cope with that; consumer tablets do not.
  • Tenants' homes and communal areas. Wet bathrooms, damp cupboards under stairs, bin stores, plant rooms and stairwells.
  • Void properties. Dust from stripping out, no heating in winter, no lighting in the back rooms, and nowhere clean to put anything down.
  • Vans and drops. Onto the kerb getting out, off the dashboard on a speed bump, onto a tiled kitchen floor while measuring up a replacement unit.
  • Dead batteries at two o'clock. A shift that starts with a device at 60% because the last operative did not charge it, and a form that cannot be completed until it is.

What actually matters in a spec

Sealing against water and dust. Look for IP67 or IP68, not "splash resistant". The first digit covers dust (6 is dust tight, which is what a void needs), the second water. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the difference. IP65, which the Windows models in our range carry, is dust tight and jet proof but not rated for immersion.

A removable battery. For a shared fleet this is the single most useful feature. Each van carries a charged spare, a flat device is back in service in a minute, and a worn battery after two years is a part to replace rather than a reason to replace the tablet. Our guide to removable batteries lists which models have one.

A camera with a flash. Damp and mould evidence, before and after photos, gas and electrical compliance photos, and the state of a void at handover all get taken in poor light. A rear flash matters more than a megapixel count. Our guide to site photos that hold up covers framing, dating and backing them up.

4G built in. Job updates, photos and signatures need to leave the property, not sit on the device until it reaches Wi-Fi. Dual SIM helps where one network is weak on an estate.

A screen that fits the forms. An 8 inch screen fills them in without pinching and zooming and still fits a van door pocket. Supervisors and surveyors doing stock condition surveys or reading drawings want 10 inches.

Android, almost always. Repairs and asset management apps are published for Android and iOS or run in a browser. Windows is only worth its extra cost where a specific desktop program forces it. Our pages under works with match the published device requirements of apps such as Totalmobile, Joblogic and BigChange to our devices, with sources. We do not claim any app is tested on them: trial your own app on one device before you order the fleet.

How do we keep tenant data safe on a shared device?

Set a screen lock with a short auto lock, keep photos syncing to an account the organisation controls rather than the device, and use your mobile device management tool to wipe a lost unit. Our guide to protecting customer data on a work tablet covers the basics in plain English.
