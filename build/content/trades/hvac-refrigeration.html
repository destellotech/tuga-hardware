Air conditioning, heat pump and refrigeration work moves a device between the worst places in a building: the roof, the plant room and the cold room, with the van in between. A consumer tablet does one of those on a good day. The job needs all three, every day.

What kills devices in HVAC and refrigeration work

  • Heat on the roof. Condensers sit on flat roofs and south facing walls. A tablet left on a warm unit casing or a van dashboard in summer cooks, and a dim screen is unreadable in the glare off a white membrane roof.
  • Cold rooms and freezer stores. Walking from a warm kitchen into a cold room and back again puts condensation on and inside anything that is not sealed, and cold eats battery capacity while you are in there.
  • Condensate, wash downs and refrigerant oil. Drip trays overflow, coils get washed, and hands are often wet or oily when the tablet comes out for a reading.
  • Plant rooms. Dust, vibration, poor light and little flat space. Things get balanced on pipework and ductwork and then fall off them.

What actually matters in a spec

Sealing against water and dust. IP67 or IP68 means dust tight and rated for immersion, which covers a drip tray and a wash down. IP65 is dust tight and rated for water jets but not immersion. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the digits. None of our devices is rated IP69K, so none should be pressure washed with the coils.

A listed operating temperature range. Cold rooms and rooftops are the two ends of it. Check what the manufacturer lists rather than assuming: in our range the A10 and W8 list -20°C to 60°C and the A8 lists no figure, so treat the A8 as a van and plant room device that visits the cold room rather than one that lives in it. Our guides to cold weather and battery life and summer heat and sun cover both ends.

A bright screen. On a roof, brightness decides whether you can read a pressure reading or a fault code at all. Look for a figure in nits. The A8 lists 1000 nits, the W8 550 and the A10 350.

Battery for a day of callouts. A removable battery means a charged spare takes over on a long day rather than a hunt for a socket in a plant room. A big fixed battery is the other answer. Either way, a van dock or car charger keeps it topped up between jobs.

Android unless a program forces Windows. Job management, service sheets, commissioning forms and photos run on Android or in a browser. Some manufacturers' diagnostic or commissioning software for controllers and heat pumps is Windows only, usually with a USB interface lead. Check with the manufacturer which platforms it supports before you decide, and read our Android or Windows guide for the five minute test.

Ports. If you plug into controllers or an analyser by USB lead, the W8 has a USB-A port and the Android models take the £8 USB-C to USB-A OTG Adapter; see our ports guide. Every device in the range lists NFC for asset tags.

Questions HVAC and refrigeration engineers ask

Will it survive a cold room?

The A10 and W8 list operating ranges down to -20°C; the A8 lists no figure, so keep it inside your jacket between readings rather than on a shelf in the store. Expect the battery percentage to drop in the cold and recover as the device warms up, as our cold weather guide explains.

Can I read it on a roof in summer?

The A8's 1000 nit screen is the one in our range made for that. The W8 at 550 nits copes with bright shade; the A10 at 350 nits wants shade or a tilted screen. Do not leave any of them on a hot casing or a dashboard, and let a hot device cool before charging it. Our screen brightness guide and summer guide have the detail.

Does the app work with no signal in the plant room?

That depends on the app, not the tablet. Most job management apps keep forms and photos on the device and sync when signal returns; check your own app's offline mode. Dual SIM 4G on the A8 and A10 lets you carry two networks for patchy sites.
