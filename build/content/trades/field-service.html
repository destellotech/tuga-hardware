Field service work is driving, diagnosing and documenting. The job comes in on the tablet, the manual is on it, and the customer signs on it before you leave.

What kills devices in field service

  • Plant rooms and boiler cupboards. Heat, oil, grease and the occasional leak. A device put down on top of a unit or on a wet floor gets whatever is there.
  • Van life. Hours on a dashboard in summer sun, a cold start on a winter morning, and being thrown on the passenger seat between jobs.
  • Flat batteries at the wrong moment. The last job of the day is when the customer wants to sign and the tablet is on 3%.
  • Drops. Off a ladder, off a roof hatch, out of the van door. The usual.
  • Weak signal. Basements, rural sites and steel-framed buildings all make uploading a job report harder than it should be.

What actually matters in a spec

A screen you can read in the van and in the sun. Brightness matters more than resolution here. The A8 runs at 800 nits, which is comfortably readable outdoors.

Battery and in-vehicle charging. A big battery plus a way to charge on the move means the device is full at every job.

Sealing and drop testing. IP68 and MIL-STD-810H cover the dust, water and drops that come with the job. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the difference between the two sealing grades.

Your software decides the operating system. Most job management apps run on Android or in a browser. Some diagnostic and commissioning tools are Windows only, and if yours is, that settles it. Otherwise Android costs noticeably less for the same job.
