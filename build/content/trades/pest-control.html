Pest control is a trade of many short visits in unpleasant places. The device that records them is handled in a loft with one hand on a joist, under a kitchen unit by torchlight and beside a drain with gloves on. A consumer phone in a case manages the paperwork; the dust, the damp and the drops finish it off.

What kills devices in pest control work

  • Lofts, voids and crawl spaces. Dark, dusty and cramped. Devices get balanced on a joist or a tank lid and then slide off it onto the ceiling below.
  • Drains, bin stores and plant rooms. Wet floors, dripping pipes and a device put down on whatever surface is nearest. Enough moisture, often enough, kills a phone in a soft case.
  • Gloves and dirty hands. Nitrile gloves, dust from insulation, grease from a commercial kitchen extract. Buttons and ports clog, and a screen becomes hard to read through the smears.
  • Dozens of short visits a day. In and out of the van, into a pocket, out again at the next site. The sheer number of handlings is what wears a consumer device out.

What actually matters in a spec

Sealing first. Look for IP67 or IP68 rather than "splash resistant". The first digit is dust and a 6 means dust tight, which is what matters in a loft. The second digit is water: IP67 is rated for a metre of immersion for 30 minutes, so a device that goes into a wet gully is within the rating, and a wipe down with a damp cloth between premises is nothing to worry about. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains the ratings. No device in our range is rated IP69K, so do not jet wash any of them.

A scanner, if your bait points are tagged. Many pest control systems put a barcode or QR label on each bait station and trap, so the technician scans it to log the check. A phone camera can read a QR code, but a built-in 2D scan engine with a physical scan key is faster and works with a glove on. Our guide to rugged tablet barcode scanners covers the difference. If your system uses NFC tags instead, every Tuga model lists NFC; our NFC guide explains what it is and is not.

A camera with a flash. Evidence photos are taken where the light is worst: droppings behind a freezer, gnaw marks under a sink, a gap in the proofing at the back of a loft. A rear camera with a flash matters more here than the megapixel count. Our guide to site photos that hold up covers framing, naming and backing them up.

Pocket size and weight. A technician on a round handles the device at every visit, so a handheld in a belt holster beats a tablet left in the van. A tablet earns its place for long audit reports and bait point plans.

A battery that lasts the round. Twenty visits with photos and scanning at each is a long day. A removable battery means a charged spare takes over rather than a hunt for a 12V socket.

Android is fine. Pest control reporting apps run on Android or in a browser, and Android is the cheaper option. Check the app's minimum Android version against the device before you buy, and trial it on one unit first.

Questions pest control technicians ask

Will my pest control reporting app run on it?

If the app is on Google Play and supports the device's Android version, or runs in a web browser, it should. The A6 runs Android 15, the A8 Android 12 and the A10 Android 14, so check the minimum version your app asks for and trial it on one device before equipping the team. We do not claim any app is tested on our devices.

Can the A6 scan the barcodes on my bait stations?

It has a built-in 2D scan engine, which reads 1D barcodes and 2D codes such as QR, with two side scan keys. Whether your app accepts input from the built-in scanner rather than only from the camera depends on the app, so check with your software provider. If your stations use NFC tags, every Tuga model lists NFC.

Handheld or tablet?

If the day is mostly scanning, photographing and ticking off checks, the handheld. If it is mostly writing and drawing, the tablet. Some firms give technicians the A6 and managers the A8 or A10. Our guide on rugged phone or rugged tablet goes through the trade offs.

Can I wipe it down between food premises?

A wipe with a damp cloth is well within an IP67 rating on the A6 and A8. Do not pressure wash any device or soak it in cleaning chemicals.
