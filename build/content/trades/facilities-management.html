In-house maintenance is a different job from field service. Nobody drives between customers. The same team covers the same buildings every day, the work comes from a CMMS, and the device is picked off a dock at the start of a shift and put back at the end, often by someone else.

What kills devices in facilities work

  • Plant rooms. Heat from boilers and air handling units, dust, condensate, and nowhere clean to put anything down.
  • Roofs and basements. Rain and wind on one job, damp and poor light on the next. Devices get rested on parapets and plant frames and knocked off.
  • Shared use. A device that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. It gets dropped more and charged less.
  • No signal below ground. Basement plant rooms and lift motor rooms are where Wi-Fi and 4G both give up, so the app has to cope with working offline and syncing later.

What actually matters in a spec

Your CMMS decides the operating system. Most CMMS mobile apps are Android or browser based, and some have no Windows app at all. A few legacy maintenance systems only run on Windows. Find out which yours is before anything else, because it settles the choice. Our guide to Android or Windows for a rugged tablet walks through it. We have checked the published requirements for several CMMS platforms, including MaintainX, Limble CMMS and IBM Maximo Mobile, and the full list is on the works with page. Those pages compare what each vendor publishes against what our devices list. They are not a claim that any app has been tested on our hardware, so run your own CMMS on one unit before you buy ten.

Asset tags. Every Tuga model lists NFC, which suits tap-to-identify asset tags. For barcodes and QR labels, the A6 has a built-in 2D scan engine and every other model scans through the camera, which is fine for a handful of tags a day and slow for a stock count. Scanner versions of the WH6, W8 and W10 exist if volumes are high. Our barcode scanner guide covers when the camera stops being enough.

Sealing. IP65 covers dust and water jets from any direction. IP67 and IP68 add immersion. None of our devices is rated IP69K, so none should be pressure washed.

A dock, not a cable. Shared devices get charged when charging is easy. A drop-in dock at the maintenance office means the tablet is full at handover without anyone hunting for a lead.

A camera with a flash. Meter readings and before and after photos happen in dark cupboards and ceiling voids.

Questions facilities teams ask

Can several people share one device?

Physically, yes: a dock for charging, a screen protector, and a strap or holster. How logins work is down to your CMMS. Some vendors publish a shared-device or kiosk mode, others expect each technician to sign in and out of the app. Check what yours offers, and read our guide to buying rugged tablets for a small team for a setup checklist and why a spare is worth having.

Will it read our asset tags?

NFC tags, yes on every model, provided your CMMS app supports NFC on your platform. Barcode and QR labels are read by the camera on the A10, WH6 and W10, or by a built-in 2D scanner on the A6 and on the scanner versions of the WH6, W8 and W10.
