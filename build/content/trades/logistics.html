A delivery device gets used more times in a day than almost any other piece of work kit. Out of the cab, scan, photo, signature, back in the cab, repeat. Whatever you choose has to cope with that rhythm, not just with being dropped once.

What kills devices in logistics

  • Cab vibration. Hours of it, every day, in a cheap suction mount. Mounts fail, devices fall into footwells, and consumer phones gradually shake loose inside.
  • Cold mornings. A phone left in the cab overnight in January can struggle to hold charge first thing.
  • Rain on the doorstep. Proof of delivery signatures happen outside, in whatever the weather is doing.
  • Constant drops. Onto pavements, tail lifts, warehouse floors. Not once a month, but several times a week.
  • Battery drain. Navigation, mobile data and the camera all running at once will flatten a normal phone before the round is finished.

What actually matters in a spec

Size. For drivers, a device that goes in a pocket or a holster beats a tablet. You need both hands for parcels.

Sealing. IP67 handles rain, puddles and a drop into standing water, which covers doorstep work. The A6 is IP67 and rated for 1.5 metre drops. None of our devices is rated IP69K for jet washing, so do not pressure wash them. Our IP68 vs IP67 guide explains what each rating actually tests.

Battery. Navigation, uploads and the camera all day need a big battery, or a removable one so a charged spare can take over mid round.

Operating temperature. The A6 is rated from -20°C to 50°C, which covers an unheated cab on a winter morning. In summer, do not leave it baking on the dashboard in direct sun.

Your software. Most delivery and proof of delivery apps run on Android. If your warehouse management system needs Windows on the handheld, that changes the answer, and the price.
